# Source coverage and learning value

Use for new lessons and retrospective audits. Explain source content, not just
teacher annotations. Read all sections of the supplied pages: unmarked reading,
tables, captions, examples and vocabulary lists as well as corrections.

## Boundary and internal coverage matrix

Identify current materials and exact assigned book pages. An archived pass, a
whole attached textbook or a mentioned range without the pages does not prove
that every exercise was assigned or taught. Distinguish supplied study material,
confirmed classroom activity, optional material and mandatory homework.

In the source audit map each section or coherent page range:

| Source and section | Lesson explanation | Vocabulary | Revision decision | Gaps |
|---|---|---|---|---|

Use paths/anchors and short decisions, not a second copy of the lesson. Record
exclusions such as optional song, unassigned neighbouring exercise, illegible
fragment or unavailable audio. Resolve omissions against the original; OCR and
previous completeness claims are not proof. For a multi-lesson audit list every
lesson and distinguish sources reopened now from earlier checks and remaining
unverified material. Checking headings alone is not a full course audit.

## Detailed teaching, selective revision

- Explain meaning, form selection, contrasts, a translated example and a likely
  beginner mistake. Do not replace a needed explanation with a link or table alone.
- For a central reading/dialogue, include the complete relevant passage with
  adjacent pronunciation and translation in manageable utterances. Explain useful
  expressions and small connecting words, not only highlighted nouns.
- Present coherent semantic groups together. Known dictionary entries may still
  need a recap in this lesson; avoid scattering essential members across lessons.
- Completeness is coverage of the verified scope, not a target number of words
  or invented exercises. Do not claim that supplied material was all taught orally.
- Review `scripts/mobile-data/core.json` for reusable essentials and
  `questions.txt` for conversational models. Improve existing cards first; add
  only topics worth regular repetition. Never copy the entire lesson/dictionary
  into cards or shrink fonts to fit. Explain why no addition is needed if so.
- Rebuild affected A4/phone outputs from shared data only when their contents
  change. Inspect final rendering and preserve stable links.

### Lesson cards and cross-course cards

The cross-course set selects and generalizes verified lesson material; it must
not be the only place where an essential word or rule is taught. Before promoting
an item, locate its explanation in a lesson and its appropriate lesson revision
card. Keep essential semantic pairs together (for example husband/wife), especially
when required by that lesson's homework. Repair an omission in the existing lesson
card before promoting it; do not add cards or reproduce the entire dictionary.
Previously learned background may link to an earlier lesson card rather than be
copied into every new lesson. Record these mappings in the internal source audit.
Check both A4 and phone outputs, and inspect the actual card before giving a learner
a link: a matching title alone does not prove that the requested material is there.

## Vocabulary and learner access

Compare reading/exercise words against all dictionaries. Distinguish missing
lemma, known lemma, new meaning/plural, name and incidental nonessential text.
Keep noun article/plural, verb first-person singular and all transcriptions.

Check the current-lesson trainer filter. A word stored in a later archived lesson
is not accessible today just because a global search finds it. Move a verified
entry to its earliest supported current lesson without duplicates, preserving
identifier/progress and repairing links/totals. Do not invent first occurrence
when the evidence is incomplete.

Regenerate dictionary/data and update index totals. Check generator counts,
cross-links, lesson layout, strict build and scoped diff. Keep the report internal;
give the learner the improved lesson and useful materials.
